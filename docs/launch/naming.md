# The name Citta

Suggested product explanation:

> Citta (चित्त, pronounced roughly “chit-ta”) is a Sanskrit word for mind or thought. The name reflects the idea behind the project: your agent can bring together the capabilities of your devices to carry out one task.

The lexical meaning is supported by Charles R. Lanman's *A Sanskrit Reader*, vocabulary page 156, hosted by the University of Cologne's Sanskrit lexicon project. [Dictionary scan](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/LANScan/LANScanpdf/lan_188.pdf).

The pronunciation is an English approximation. Sanskrit transliteration uses **c** for the sound approximated by English **ch**. [Routledge pronunciation reference](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/overview/indian-and-tibetan-philosophy/v-1/sections/pronunciation-of-sanskrit-words).

Use **mind or thought**, rather than asserting a single universal philosophical definition such as consciousness. Different traditions develop the term differently; the Yoga tradition, for example, distinguishes citta from consciousness. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yoga/).

The connection to devices working together is Citta's product metaphor. It is not a literal Sanskrit translation of “distributed intelligence,” a claim that computers are conscious, or a documented anecdote about the maker's personal history.
